# Interview Guide: Tournament Organizers

**Length:** 45 minutes  **Participants:** organizers or store staff who run Lorcana events
**Answers:** RQ1, RQ2, RQ4, RQ5

## Tips for the interviewer

- Ask about **what happened at real, recent events**, not what they'd do in theory.
  "Tell me about the last time…" gives better answers than "Would you ever…?"
- Don't suggest answers. Ask "How did that go?", not "Was that frustrating?"
- When something sounds important, dig in: "Can you tell me more?", "What happened
  next?", "Why do you think that was?"
- Stay quiet after they finish an answer; people often add the most useful detail
  after a pause.
- **Don't show the app until the end.** Showing it early steers the conversation
  toward your solution instead of their problems.
- Write down exact words for strong statements; they make good quotes in the
  findings.

## 1. Introduction and consent (3 min)

> Thanks for meeting with me. I'm researching how local Lorcana tournaments are run,
> to design software that makes running them easier. There are no right or wrong
> answers; I'm here to learn from your experience.
>
> I'll take notes. Your name won't appear in anything I share; you'll be identified
> only as "Organizer 1" or similar. Is it OK if I take notes? *(If recording:)* Is it
> OK if I record audio, only for my own notes? You can skip any question or stop at
> any time.

Note the consent answer at the top of your notes.

## 2. Background (5 min)

1. How long have you been running Lorcana events? Which other games have you run
   events for?
2. What kinds of Lorcana events do you run, and how often?
3. How many players come to a typical event? What's the biggest you've run?
4. Who else helps you run events?

## 3. Walk through the last event (15 min)

> Think about the most recent event you ran. Take me through it from the time you
> started preparing until you went home.

Let them talk, then fill gaps with these probes:

- **Before the event:** How did players sign up? What did you prepare beforehand?
- **Registration:** How did you check people in? What happened with late arrivals?
- **Pairings:** What did you use to pair each round? How did players find out where
  to sit?
- **Results:** How were results reported to you? How did you enter them?
- **Between rounds:** How long did it take from the last result to the next pairings?
  What were players doing while they waited?
- **Standings and tiebreakers:** How did you work out standings? Did anyone question
  them?
- **Top cut:** How did you decide the cut and run the bracket?
- **Timer:** How did you time rounds? What happened when time was called?
- **After the event:** What did you have to report, and to whom? What did you do with
  the results afterwards?

## 4. Problems and workarounds (10 min)

5. What went wrong at that event, even small things?
6. Tell me about the worst event you've run. What happened?
7. Have you ever had a wrong pairing, a wrong result or a standings mistake? How did
   you find out, and how did you fix it?
8. What workarounds have you come up with? (Spreadsheets, notes, rules of thumb.)
9. What part of running an event takes the most time? *(Ask for a rough number of
   minutes.)*
10. What part causes the most stress?

## 5. Data and reporting (5 min)

11. Do you keep any records from past events? What do you use them for?
12. If you could see one report about your events, what would it show?
    *(Probe: attendance over time, new vs. returning players, popular decks.)*
13. Does the store owner ask you for anything after events?
14. How would players feel about having their records or decks tracked? Has anyone
    raised that?

## 6. Setup and constraints (3 min)

15. What device do you run events on? Is there reliable Wi-Fi at the store?
16. Is there a screen or projector? A printer?
17. Are there official rules or reporting requirements you have to follow?

## 7. Priorities (3 min)

> If you could fix only **three** things about running events, what would they be?

Then: "If you had $100 to spend across those three fixes, how would you split it?"
This shows relative importance, not just a list.

## 8. App feedback and wrap-up (optional, 5 min)

Show the app and give them a task: "Start an event with these 8 players and pair
round 1." Watch without helping. Note where they hesitate or get stuck.

> Is there anything I didn't ask that I should have? Would you be willing to test an
> early version at one of your events? Who else should I talk to?

Thank them.
